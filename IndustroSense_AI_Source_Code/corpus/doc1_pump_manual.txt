TITLE: Centrifugal Pump Model CP-204 - Operation and Maintenance Manual (Excerpt, Section 7: Vibration and Bearing Faults)

7.1 Overview
The CP-204 centrifugal pump uses a horizontally mounted rotor supported by two grease-lubricated ball bearings. Excessive vibration is one of the most common field complaints and is frequently linked to bearing wear, shaft misalignment, or cavitation.

7.2 Diagnosing High Vibration
If overall vibration velocity exceeds 7.1 mm/s RMS (ISO 10816 Zone C or worse), technicians should first check for the following, in order:
1. Coupling alignment - verify angular and parallel offset are within 0.05 mm using a dial indicator or laser alignment tool.
2. Bearing condition - listen for grinding or high-pitched whine; a spectrum analysis peak near the bearing defect frequency indicates race or ball damage.
3. Cavitation - a crackling or "gravel" sound near the suction flange, combined with reduced discharge pressure, suggests insufficient Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH).
4. Loose foundation bolts or baseplate grouting failure.

7.3 Bearing Replacement Procedure
Bearings should be replaced every 18,000 operating hours or immediately if defect-frequency vibration amplitude exceeds 4.5 mm/s. Use only manufacturer-approved bearing part number BRG-6205-2RS. Do not reuse removed bearings.

7.4 Common Fault Codes
- CP204-E11: High vibration, non-drive-end bearing
- CP204-E12: High vibration, drive-end bearing
- CP204-E20: Suction cavitation warning
- CP204-E31: Seal leakage detected

7.5 Safety Note
Always lock out and tag out (LOTO) the motor supply before removing the coupling guard or bearing housing.
